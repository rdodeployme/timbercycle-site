Timbercycle › Your industry › Ports & shipping

Timber off
disembarked freight.

Every container that comes off a ship brings timber with it, and almost none of it goes back. Pallets, skids, crating and dunnage accumulate at a rate no general waste contract was ever designed for.

Build a program Book a site assessment
The situation

The pile is predictable. That's the opportunity.

Port and terminal timber has a specific profile: high volume, mixed sizes, a lot of it plain and sound, and a lot of it export-grade hardwood that is worth reconditioning rather than burying. It also arrives in bursts, not evenly, which is why bins overflow and yards fill up between collections.

A program built for that profile runs on the freight rhythm, not on a fixed bin schedule. Equipment sits where the timber lands, collections flex with vessel arrivals, and grading happens at our yard so terminal staff never touch a pallet twice.
