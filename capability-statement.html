Who we are

Timbercycle is a timber recovery and pallet recycling business operating within Recycle Group. Our processing site is at 116 Furner Avenue, North Geelong, Victoria. We handle timber only — pallets, skids, crates, dunnage, packing timber, offcuts and oversize timber for chipping.

What we do

Stream 01 — Recovery and reuse. Pallets are collected, assessed for structural integrity, graded and repaired where viable, then returned to the supply chain as reconditioned stock.

Stream 02 — Recycling. Material beyond safe repair is shredded and processed into recycled mulch, oversize chip, timber fibre for paper and board manufacturing, and biomass or pellet feedstock.

Service scope

  • Scheduled bulk pallet collection
  • On-call and ad-hoc collection
  • Collection equipment supplied on site
  • Multi-site program management
  • Grading and condition reporting
  • In-yard processing at North Geelong
  • Mobile and contract on-site processing
  • Transfer station timber recovery
  • Construction and demolition timber
  • Supply of reconditioned pallets and mulch

Materials accepted

Timber pallets of all sizes including Australian standard, Euro, export and custom-built; timber skids and bearers; export crates and timber boxes; dunnage and packing timber; timber offcuts and broken stock; oversize timber for chipping. Treated, painted, laminated and engineered timbers are assessed case by case.

Not accepted: asbestos and hazardous waste.

Coverage

Victoria. North Geelong base, servicing Melbourne metropolitan and regional Victoria. Interstate expansion is planned; we do not claim coverage we are not currently delivering.

Reporting

Volumes collected, reused and diverted from landfill are recorded per site and per period, and provided to clients for internal and sustainability reporting.

Commercial structure

Programs are priced against each site's pallet volume, condition mix and location following a site assessment. We do not quote a flat rate ahead of assessing the material, because the reusable proportion of a site's pallets materially changes the economics for both parties.
